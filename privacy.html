1. What this policy covers

The foundation works to make European digital infrastructure easier to discover, understand, and compare. This policy explains how we handle personal information when you visit infrastructure.eu, correspond with us, or make a one-off donation. It also covers related administration and optional public acknowledgement of donors.

2. Who is responsible

Stichting i12e.eu, a Dutch foundation publicly known as the European Digital Infrastructure Foundation, is the legal entity responsible for the processing described here. It is the data controller under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). In this policy, “we”, “us”, and “our” refer to Stichting i12e.eu.

3. Website visits and browser storage

Our hosting and delivery providers, GitHub Pages and Cloudflare, process technical information to deliver and protect the website. This includes IP addresses, requested pages and resources, request times, browser information, and security or error records. Referring addresses may also be included. Cloudflare receives network-error reports from supporting browsers to help maintain website reliability.

Website pages load shared design assets from design.i12e.eu, hosted on GitHub Pages, and fonts from Google Fonts. These requests disclose your IP address and relevant browser and request information to the service delivering the resource.

We use your browser’s local storage to remember display and reading preferences. These values are preferences, not visitor identifiers. They have no programmed expiry and remain until replaced or removed. You can remove them by clearing the site’s data in your browser.

Cloudflare may use security cookies or similar mechanisms to protect the website; see its cookie information. We do not use advertising trackers or a separate behavioural-analytics service.

4. Correspondence and administration

If you contact us, we use the name and contact details you provide, your message and attachments, and correspondence metadata such as dates and delivery details to respond and manage follow-up. Related administration may also involve organisation details, supplier invoices, and payment information.

Providing enquiry information is voluntary, but we need enough information about your request and a way to reach you to reply. Please avoid sending unnecessary sensitive personal details.

We do not measure whether recipients open our correspondence or click its links.

5. One-off donations

If you donate through a Mollie Payment Link, the link takes you to Mollie’s hosted checkout. You enter payment information there or with the bank or payment provider for your chosen method. Donations are one-off payments, without recurring charges, subscriptions, or donation invoices.

Mollie processes payment and transaction details, your IP address, and browser and device information. Depending on the payment method and information supplied, it may also process your name, address, email address, or telephone number. The payment cannot be completed without the information required for the selected method.

We can access the donation’s amount, currency, date, reference, status, and payment method, together with relevant refund or dispute information. Method-dependent payer details can include a name, bank-account details, contact information, or masked card details. Through this hosted payment-link flow, we do not receive full card numbers, card security codes, or online-banking login credentials.

We use donation records to administer donations, reconcile payments, and handle enquiries, refunds, or disputes. Where contact details are available to us, we may send a personal thank-you or acknowledgement. Donating does not subscribe you to a mailing list or give consent to unrelated marketing.

With your separate consent, we may publish your name to acknowledge your donation. Public acknowledgement is optional and does not affect your donation. You can withdraw consent as explained under your rights.

6. Why we process information

We rely on the following legal bases under the GDPR:

  • Website delivery, presentation, and security: our legitimate interests in making the foundation’s information available and readable, maintaining reliable access, investigating faults, and protecting the site against misuse (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Enquiries and related follow-up: our legitimate interests in communicating with people who contact the foundation and handling its ordinary administration (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Executing an individual’s donation: performing the donation agreement you request, to the extent processing is necessary to receive the contribution and make related transaction corrections (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Other donation administration: our legitimate interests in sending personal thank-you messages, communicating with representatives of organisational donors, preventing fraud, resolving disputes, and establishing, exercising, or defending legal claims (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Public acknowledgement of individual donors: your separate consent to publishing your name (Article 6(1)(a)).
  • Mandatory accounting records and data-rights requests: compliance with applicable legal obligations, including Dutch recordkeeping requirements for donations and obligations under data protection law (Article 6(1)(c)).

8. How long information is kept

We review the personal information we hold and remove it when it is no longer needed for its purpose, taking account of legal obligations or claims:

  • Correspondence: whether the enquiry and any follow-up are complete, whether an ongoing relationship still requires the record, and whether an unresolved request, dispute, or legal obligation requires continued retention. This includes donor enquiries and personal thank-you messages.
  • Public acknowledgement: we keep your name published while the acknowledgement serves its purpose and your consent remains in place.
  • Accounting and supporting records, including donation transactions and reconciliation records: the applicable Dutch statutory retention period, generally seven years after a record ceases to have current administrative relevance. Longer periods apply where required by law. This does not automatically cover all donor correspondence or optional contact details. See the Dutch Tax Administration’s retention guidance for foundations.
  • Technical and security records: the providers’ applicable retention criteria for delivery, troubleshooting, security, and legal requirements, as described in their linked privacy notices.

Mollie applies its own statutory and purpose-based retention requirements, which may prevent deletion of some payment records following a request. See Mollie’s retention explanation.

9. International processing

Some processing takes place outside the European Economic Area (EEA).

Our email provider, Proton Mail, stores server data in Switzerland, Germany, or Norway according to its privacy policy. Switzerland benefits from a European Commission adequacy decision, recognising its level of data protection for transfers from the EEA.

GitHub, Cloudflare, and Google operate internationally, including in the United States. Their published arrangements describe reliance on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework for covered transfers and European Commission standard contractual clauses for other relevant transfers. Details are in GitHub’s transfer information, Cloudflare’s data processing terms, and Google’s transfer frameworks.

Our accounting service, Odoo, uses a European production hosting region. Its published arrangements describe international support access and possible backups outside the EEA, including standard contractual clauses for transfers involving international staff access.

Mollie states that third parties involved in its services may process personal information outside the EEA. It describes applicable transfer arrangements, including European Commission standard contractual clauses where relevant. See the cross-border transfer section of Mollie’s privacy statement.

Use our privacy contact details to request more information or a copy of applicable transfer safeguards.

10. Your rights and complaints

Subject to the conditions in data protection law, you can ask to access your personal information, correct inaccurate information, have information erased, or restrict its processing. Required accounting records may need to be retained despite an erasure request.

You can object to processing based on our legitimate interests. We will stop this processing unless we can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights, and freedoms, or need it to establish, exercise, or defend legal claims. You also have a right to data portability where its conditions apply: it concerns information you provided that is processed automatically on the basis of consent or a contract.

You can withdraw consent to public acknowledgement at any time. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing before you withdrew consent.

For privacy questions, rights requests, or withdrawal of consent, email privacy@infrastructure.eu or write to the postal address above. Requests are normally free. If we have reasonable doubts about your identity, we may ask for information necessary to confirm it.

For information Mollie processes as an independent controller, contact privacy@mollie.com. Its privacy statement explains its handling of rights requests.

We will respond without undue delay and within one month of receiving your request. Where the complexity or number of requests justifies an extension, the deadline may be extended by up to two further months; we will explain the extension within the first month. If a legal exception prevents us from acting on a request, we will explain the reason and how you can challenge it.

You may complain to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, the Dutch data protection authority, or to the competent supervisory authority where you habitually live, work, or believe an infringement occurred. You do not have to contact us before making a complaint.

The foundation does not make decisions about individuals solely by automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects. Mollie separately describes automated checks to detect fraud and financial crime, and rights to human review and to contest qualifying automated decisions, in the automated decision-making section of its privacy statement.

11. Security

The website uses HTTPS to protect information in transit between your browser and the service. Access to correspondence and accounting records is limited to authorised people who need it for their work.
